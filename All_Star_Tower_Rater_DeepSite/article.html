How Russia’s Government Tightens Its Grip on the Web

Since 2020, the Russian government has cracked down on internet freedom, banning VPNs and enforcing censorship. Yandex, the country's leading search engine, has been forced to align with the Kremlin's control, altering search results. This is part of Russia's strategy to isolate its internet and increase control over online activity.

Isolating Russia: The Laws That Bind the Internet

On September 21st, 2020, a bill was introduced in the Kremlin making the use of various internet protocols like HTTPS and DNS illegal, labeling them as criminal activities. By 2024, a law was passed banning VPNs, marking a significant escalation in Russia's efforts to create a tightly controlled, national internet. These actions are part of a broader strategy to isolate Russia's digital sphere from the rest of the world, with the Kremlin aiming to strengthen its ability to monitor and control online communication, limiting access to foreign information and reinforcing national security measures.

Yandex Under Pressure: Navigating Government Contro 1

Yandex is russian company knows thanks his browser create in 1997, use by 72% of russian internet users, while Google only 28% However, the Kremlin gradually took control of it. In 2019, under pressure, Yandex was forced to create a "governance structure" that gave the government veto power over certain decisions. Indeed, there is a manipulation of search results on the browser : Independent or critical sources of the Kremlin are either suppressed or relegated to a lower position in the results. State-controlled media (like Russia Today or TASS) are highlighted. Certain keywords related to the war in Ukraine (such as "invasion" or "war crimes") are filtered.

Yandex Faces Internal Struggles Amid Growing Censorship

After the invasion of Ukraine, Yandex faced internal pushback. Some employees, including executives, were troubled by the Kremlin’s growing control over their activities. The pressure to comply with government demands on censorship led to a tense atmosphere within the company. This situation highlights the struggle of local businesses under the Kremlin’s increasing influence, limiting their freedom and forcing them to align with state policies. Yandex's case is an example of how Russia’s tech sector is being constrained by government pressure.
